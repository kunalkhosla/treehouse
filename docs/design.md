# Design — Phase 1 platform

## Principle: post-assisted, not tree-hung

One ~17.5" tree on a slope. The platform stands on **6 pressure-treated posts on concrete footings**; the **trunk passes through a generous hole** in the deck. Nothing structural is bolted to the tree in Phase 1.

Why:
- Trees **sway and grow** — rigid attachments tear out or strangle the tree.
- A single tree isn't enough to safely carry a platform on its own.
- Posts absorb the slope and make the build **infinitely extensible** later.
- Far more forgiving for a first-time builder.

(A proper tree attachment with TABs/limb bolts is a possible Phase 3 upgrade, not now.)

## Dimensions

- **Platform:** 8 ft × 8 ft, deck dead level.
- **Slope:** 15° → tan(15°) × 96 in = **~26 in drop** across the 8 ft uphill→downhill run.
- **Deck height (set level):**
  - Downhill edge: **~4.5 ft (54 in)** above grade → real treehouse height.
  - Uphill edge: 54 − 26 = **~28 in (2.3 ft)** above grade → easy step-up for little kids.
- **Tree opening:** **26 in × 26 in** framed hole, centered. Trunk 17.5" → leaves ~4" gap all around for growth + sway. Never let framing touch bark.

## Framing scheme

Beams run **cross-slope** (along the uphill and downhill edges). Joists run **uphill→downhill**, spanning the 8 ft between the two beams. The tree opening sits mid-span and is framed with doubled headers + trimmer joists.

- **Posts:** 6 × 6×6 PT — 3 on the uphill beam line, 3 on the downhill beam line, spaced 0 / 4 / 8 ft.
- **Beams:** 2 × doubled 2×8 PT (each beam = two plies bolted together), 8 ft long, supported at 3 posts (two 4 ft spans — very strong). Beams sit in post-top saddles / bolted to posts.
- **Joists:** 2×8 PT @ 16" on-center, 8 ft span (very conservative for a 2×8 — handles kid impact loads easily). Joist hangers into beams.
- **Tree opening:** double the two trimmer joists either side of the hole; double the two headers top/bottom of the hole. Hangers at all cut joist ends.
- **Decking:** 5/4 × 6 PT deck boards, ~1/4" gaps.
- **Lateral bracing:** 4×4 knee braces at 45° from each post up to its beam (both directions on the tall downhill posts). Critical for racking stability on tall posts.

## Footings

- 6 poured concrete piers, **below frost line** (N. NJ: dig ~42" to be safe; frost ~36").
- 10–12" tube forms (Sonotube), or wide pier blocks if a pro signs off — **poured is recommended on a slope.**
- Simpson **ABU66** standoff post bases anchored into wet concrete (keeps post end off the ground = no rot).
- Set posts long, mark a single level line around all 6 with a laser/water level, then cut tops to land the beams level.

## Railings & access (kids — non-negotiable)

- **Railing:** 36 in min height around the full perimeter except the entry.
- **Balusters:** vertical 2×2 spaced so **no gap exceeds 4 in** (a head can't pass). Alternative: plywood half-walls with cut-out windows.
- **Access:**
  - Uphill side: 1–2 box steps (only ~28" to climb).
  - Downhill side (optional 2nd access / the "fun" entry): ladder with handrails, or a future trapdoor.

## Extensibility hooks (build these in now, cheap)

- Oversize the footings slightly and leave the downhill bay open → future sandbox/storage underneath (you get the headroom for free on the tall side).
- Keep one railing section removable → future slide or climbing-wall mount.
- Note the **second trunk** ~behind the build — future rope-bridge / zipline anchor. Site the platform with a clear sightline to it.
