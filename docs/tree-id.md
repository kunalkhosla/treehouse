# Tree identification

## Measurements

- Circumference @ 5 ft: **55 in**
- Diameter: **17.5 in** (55 / π)
- Form: tall, straight, single leader, full healthy canopy, forest-grown
- Bark: tight interlacing ridges in a diamond / net pattern, gray with heavy pale-gray lichen

## Best guess

The diamond/interlacing-ridge bark is the shared signature of two species common in NJ woods:

1. **Hickory** (pignut/mockernut, *Carya*) — leaning this way; ridges look shallow and net-like.
2. **White ash** (*Fraxinus americana*) — possible; ash diamonds are usually deeper/more regular.

Both are excellent, very strong anchor trees. **Hickory is one of the densest hardwoods in the forest.**

## How to confirm (do this)

| Clue | Hickory | Ash |
|---|---|---|
| Leaf arrangement on twig | **Alternate** (staggered) | **Opposite** (paired) |
| Leaflets per compound leaf | 5–9, end leaflet largest | 5–11 |
| Seed on ground (fall) | Hard nut in a husk | Winged "helicopter" samara |

> **Easiest tell:** opposite vs alternate branching/leaves. Ash = opposite. Hickory = alternate.

Grab a low twig/leaf photo and drop it in `docs/photos/` — we'll lock it down.

## ⚠️ EAB check — only matters IF it's ash

Emerald Ash Borer has devastated NJ ash. **Do not build a kids' platform under a declining ash.** Before building, if ash is confirmed, inspect for:

- **D-shaped exit holes** (~1/8") in the bark
- **Upper-canopy dieback** / thinning crown
- **"Blonding"** — pale patches where woodpeckers stripped bark chasing larvae
- S-shaped galleries under loose bark

Canopy currently looks full and healthy → probably fine, but verify. **If hickory: no EAB concern, proceed.**
